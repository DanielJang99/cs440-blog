## Key Idea
Conventional map services are centralized and miss indoor spaces, making them inapt for various spatial applications (e.g., 3D indoor navigation, AR, etc). OpenFLAME proposes a federated alternative in which organizations run their own map servers and clients discover them through a location-based naming layer built on top of DNS, with physical regions encoded as "geo-domains." Unlike DNS, zones are allowed to overlap so multiple providers can map the same space, and services like routing and localization are stitched together across maps.

## Critique
Reusing DNS is a novel design choice: its hierarchy, delegation, chain of trust, and caching map naturally onto map zones, and caching keeps the many lookups per discovery cheap. The evaluation is less convincing, since it runs at small scale (a single DNS server and a hand-sharded city map) and doesn't test what happens when many independent providers overlap. Questions that would decide real-world adoption are left for future work, such as how to rank and filter overlapping results, how to handle abuse and denial of service, and why organizations would publish their maps at all.

## Connections
Several earlier readings worked backward from observed behavior to design: [Misa et al.](/cs440-blog/2026/09/28/internet-invariants/) reverse-engineered the robust designs behind Internet invariants, and the [QUIC paper](/cs440-blog/2026/10/19/quic/) inferred hidden hypergiant infrastructure from incidental signals. OpenFLAME goes the other way, building federation into the design as a counterweight to the kind of consolidation the QUIC paper had to work around.
