## Key Idea
WANs are usually planned to survive a small number of failures under expected traffic, yet outages still happen when several likely failures coincide with shifts in traffic demand. The authors hence argue the importance of identifying the worst probable **degradation** to a given WAN under such failure scenarios. They propose Raha, a tool that searches for the realistic combinations of failures and traffic that would hurt the network most compared to how it performs when healthy, and alerts relevant operators in advance. 


## Critique
The most convincing idea is measuring how much failures hurt the network compared to the same network in a healthy state, rather than just finding when the network performs worst. The naive approach can be gamed by simply choosing low traffic, while Raha's comparison isolates the damage that failures actually cause.
However, Raha assumes links fail independently, so it rates multiple simultaneous failures as very unlikely, yet the outage that motivated the paper was caused by a single earthquake in Africa cutting several fibers at once. The paper says Raha can model such shared-risk groups (SRLGs), but doesn't evaluate them.


## Connections
I think Raha fits [Misa et al.'s](/cs440-blog/2026/09/28/internet-invariants/) HOT framework that systems optimized for expected conditions are fragile under unexpected ones, since Raha searches for failures and traffic shifts that WAN's design (e.g., B4) did not anticipate.
