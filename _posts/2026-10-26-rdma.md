## Key Idea

RDMA over Ethernet depends on PFC to avoid loss, but PFC causes HOL blocking and limits deployment scale (due to unfairness, deadlock). 
In addition, existing lossy-RDMA NICs mistake reordering from packet spraying for loss and fall back on slow timeouts. The authors propose **DCP**, where congested switches trim a packet's payload and forward only its header through a small, prioritized queue, so the sender learns exactly which packet was lost. Since every loss is explicitly reported, the NIC can treat other OOO arrivals as reordering and write packets directly to memory in any order.

## Critique

The FPGA prototype is convincing, since it shows the redesign costs only about 1–2% more hardware than a standard RDMA NIC (contrary to common assumption that NIC redesign is expensive).  
However, the paper can only guarantee that trimmed headers are never lost when a few senders converge on one switch port; for extreme incasts (e.g., 255 senders on a single port), header loss is still possible (though very rare in testbed simulations). 
Also missing is evidence at scale: the testbed has just two switches, and trimming requires switch support that commodity ASICs don't yet offer.


## Connections
Like [dcSim](/cs440-blog/2026/10/21/datacenter-transport/), DCP sends tiny packets through a high-priority queue to accomodate AI traffic, but it reports a loss after the fact instead of probing the path beforehand. 
In addition, DCP and [P-ECMP](/cs440-blog/2026/10/07/multi-path/) both address ECMP hash collisions, but in different ways. P-ECMP lets end hosts choose each flow's path, while DCP spreads every packet across all paths and makes the NIC tolerate the resulting reordering.
